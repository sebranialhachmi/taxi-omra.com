# 03 · Content Gap Analysis

Depth benchmarks are based on the best example among the 11 competitors for each page type.

| Page type | Best competitor benchmark | Typical competitor | Jeddah Travels plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | ~800–1,200 words, weak schema | 400–700 words | 1,000+ words, WebSite+SearchAction, LocalBusiness, BreadcrumbList |
| City page | 300–600 words (few competitors have them) | Missing | 800+ words, unique intro/routes/FAQ, JSON-LD Place |
| Airport page | 500 words on 1 airport (KAIA) | Generic | Per-terminal sections, 900+ words, Airport schema |
| Service page | 300–500 words | Thin | 700+ words per service, Service schema |
| Route page | Rare, 200–400 words | Missing | 600+ words per pair, TravelAction/Trip schema, price band |
| FAQ | Short accordion | Sometimes present | 20+ Q&A, FAQPage schema, bilingual |
| Blog | Thin, sporadic | Missing / stale | Editorial cadence, Article schema, internal links |
| Vehicle page | Basic gallery | Fleet only | Vehicle class page with capacity/luggage/price band, Product schema |

## Heading discipline

- Every page: single H1 = target query; H2 = subtopics; H3 = FAQ / details.
- Competitors frequently ship duplicate or missing H1s — this is a defensible edge.

## Duplicate content risk

- Programmatic route pages must template-vary intros, ETA, distance, price band, points-of-interest, and FAQ per pair to avoid near-duplicate footprint.
- Bilingual pages must use reciprocal `hreflang` and unique translated bodies — no auto-translated boilerplate.

## Topical authority strategy

- Own the **airport ↔ holy cities** cluster end-to-end: KAIA terminals, Makkah districts, Madinah districts, Miqat points, Taif.
- Add pillar articles: "Jeddah → Makkah transfer guide", "Umrah transport checklist", "KAIA terminal-by-terminal guide".
